# PlatePath — Market, Pricing & Projections

> Companion to [LEGAL.md](./LEGAL.md). Market-size figures come from
> research-firm summaries with divergent methodologies — treat TAM/CAGR as
> **directional**, and anchor to the hard facts (mandates, loan balances,
> adoption surveys) rather than a single headline number. Projections are a
> transparent, deterministic model (`packages/shared/src/pricing.ts`); every
> input is an editable assumption, and the numbers below are generated by
> `npm run projections`.

## 1. Is the need real? Yes — but not for the original idea

The original mechanism (find any plate via public cameras) has **no legitimate
market** — every real buyer needs *consent-based* location, not third-party
surveillance. But the underlying jobs are real and well-funded:

- **Fleets** must know where vehicles are, their routes, and driver speed — the
  **FMCSA ELD mandate** legally forces telematics onto commercial vehicles.
- **Parents:** a **2025 Pew survey found ~72% of US parents** of under-17s use at
  least one digital monitoring tool (up from 61% in 2022).
- **Dealers/lenders:** **~$32B** in BHPH loans outstanding (2025 Fed FEDS Note);
  subprime hit **15.31%** of vehicle financing in Q4 2025 (Experian) — the highest
  Q4 share since 2021. GPS collateral tracking is **already standard** here.
- **Law enforcement:** ALPR is the fastest-growing segment — but it's owned by
  Flock (~$300M ARR, ~$7.5–8.4B valuation, ~9,400 government contracts) and
  Motorola/Vigilant, and is the most legally toxic.

**SAM/SOM:** a realistic serviceable market for a new entrant is the **SMB
fleet + BHPH/subprime dealer** slice — hundreds of thousands of small fleets and
BHPH dealers, each with tens-to-hundreds of vehicles/VINs, at $9–20/unit/mo. Even
1–2% penetration of that slice is a >$50M ARR business.

## 3. Segmentation & the wedge

**Wedge = BHPH / subprime dealer collateral tracking. Then fleet. Family as
top-of-funnel. Agency gated and last.**

Why dealer/BHPH first (best startup-wedge score):
1. **Buyers concentrated & identifiable** (BHPH dealers, subprime finance cos).
2. **Pain is urgent and dollar-denominated** — a single recovery dwarfs the
   device+subscription cost, against ~$32B outstanding and rising delinquency.
3. **Built-in legal consent** — the borrower **signs a GPS agreement at
   financing**, which is exactly the statutory consent CA §637.7 / TX §16.06
   require. This is the compliance moat the consumer idea lacked.
4. **Incumbents are fragmented** (PassTime, Spireon/GoldStar, Ituran, Kahu,
   CalAmp) — no single dominant SaaS brand, and they under-invest in software UX.

**Fleet** is the natural expansion (same GPS hardware supply chain, adjacent
buyer) — but expect heavy competition from Samsara/Geotab/Motive; win on **"no
$150 hardware fee, no 36-month lock-in."** **Family** is a brand/funnel play,
monetized via annual prepay to fight churn. **Agency** is highest-margin but
sold off-store under legal process — pursue only once compliance is mature.

## 6. Three-year projections

Deterministic cohort model with an **S-curve** on the add-rate (growth tapers, no
3-year hockey stick). Run `npm run projections` to reproduce. Segments modeled
per-unit (vehicle / VIN / account).

| Scenario | Yr 1 rev | Yr 2 rev | Yr 3 rev | Exit ARR (mo 36) | Active units | SW gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | ~$0.53M | ~$1.64M | ~$2.98M | **~$3.5M** | ~18,700 | ~90% |
| **Base** | ~$1.04M | ~$3.53M | ~$6.83M | **~$8.3M** | ~43,500 | ~90% |
| **Aggressive** | ~$1.73M | ~$6.60M | ~$14.0M | **~$17.4M** | ~91,400 | ~90% |

**Base-case milestones:** M6 ~4.6K units / ~$0.84M ARR · M12 ~10K / ~$1.9M · M24
~25K / ~$4.8M · M36 ~43.5K / ~$8.3M.

**Unit economics (base):**

| Segment | ARPU | LTV | CAC | LTV:CAC | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family | $11/mo | $220 | $42 | **5.2×** | 4.2 mo |
| Fleet (per vehicle) | $20/mo | $880 | $120 | **7.3×** | 7.4 mo |
| Dealer (per VIN) | $9/mo | $562 | $45 | **12.5×** | 5.6 mo |
| Agency (per account) | $1,200/mo | $119,400 | $9,000 | **13.3×** | 7.5 mo |

**What these numbers do and don't say (read this):**

- The margin shown is **software gross margin** (revenue − serve cost). It
  **excludes** fixed operating costs — R&D/engineering, G&A, hardware logistics,
  compliance/legal. A real P&L layers those on top and will show a **net cash burn
  in Years 1–2** even though contribution is positive; that's normal and is what a
  seed/Series-A round funds.
- "Cumulative contribution" (positive by ~$5.3M in base by month 36) is
  **contribution margin**, not net profit.
- ARPU/CAC/churn are **benchmarks, not observed data** — replace them with cohort
  data after the first pilots. Set a gate: **kill consumer paid acquisition if
  blended LTV:CAC stays below ~2.5× after two cohorts**; concentrate spend on
  fleet/dealer where it should clear 3×.
- The model assumes hardware-light telematics (**~90% software margin**). If a
  segment requires subsidized hardware or licensed data, blended margin drops to
  30–50% for that segment — modeled via the `hardwareMarginPerAdd` input.

## 7. Go-to-market & roadmap

1. **Phase 1 (0–9 mo) — Dealer/BHPH wedge.** Hardwired GPS + lawful-recovery
   workflow + lienholder verification. Sell direct to BHPH dealers and small
   subprime lenders (NIADA channels, regional dealer associations). Prove
   recovery ROI. Ship the verification + audit + consent stack (already built).
2. **Phase 2 (6–18 mo) — Fleet.** Reuse the hardware supply chain and engine.
   Position against Samsara/Motive on price + no lock-in. Add ELD-readiness, API.
3. **Phase 3 (12–24 mo) — Family funnel.** Consumer app, annual-prepay-first,
   referral loops; keep it a brand/funnel play, not the revenue base.
4. **Phase 4 (18 mo+) — Agency.** Only after compliance/audit maturity; off-store,
   legal-process gated. Highest margin, slowest sales cycle.

**Moat:** the **consent-and-verification architecture** is the differentiator and
the compliance barrier — it's what lets PlatePath serve regulated buyers (BHPH,
fleet with driver disclosure, agencies) that a "type any plate" product legally
cannot touch.

## 8. Key risks

- **Legal/regulatory** (see LEGAL.md): the accelerating restrictive trend on ALPR
  and location data. Mitigation: we're not an ALPR operator; first-party consent
  model; per-state gating; counsel review.
- **Incumbent competition** in fleet (Samsara/Geotab/Motive) and dealer (Spireon/
  PassTime/Ituran). Mitigation: pricing + UX + compliance workflow; wedge in BHPH
  where incumbents are fragmented.
- **Consumer unit economics** are the weakest (high churn, low ARPU). Mitigation:
  annual prepay, don't build the base case on consumer subs.
- **Hardware/COGS** if data-licensing or devices are required at scale. Mitigation:
  model both margin worlds; keep hardware optional where phone SDK suffices.
- **CAC discipline** — enforce the LTV:CAC gates above.
